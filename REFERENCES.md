# Market After Dark — reference research
Research date: 18 November 2026. User selected a Lagos-inspired Nigerian market.

## Environmental references used
1. [Balogun Market — The Centenary Project / Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/balogun-market/1QG2UTNbByQw2A?hl=en). Reference for canopy-based stalls, lock-up shops, fabric trading and layered urban market frontage. Applied as two stall rows, fabric displays and shop buildings. This is a fictional layout, not a reconstruction of Balogun.
2. [How Lagos markets inspired Nigeria’s designers — Wallpaper, Ijeoma Ndukwe](https://www.wallpaper.com/design-interiors/how-lagos-street-markets-inspire-nigerian-creatives). Reference for umbrellas, produce baskets, corrugated roofing and timber/metal clothing-display structures. Applied as striped shelter, produce counters, umbrella stands and original fabric patterns.

Images remain on the linked publishers’ sites. No reference photographs were copied into the game or used as textures. All current market props, characters, signs and textile patterns are original procedural geometry or canvas-generated graphics. No third-party asset attribution is required for these original meshes.

## Character asset research
[Kenney character catalog](https://kenney.nl/assets?search=characters) and [animation import guidance](https://kenney.nl/knowledge-base/game-assets-3d/importing-characters-and-animations) were considered as potential future sources. An older animated-characters-1 URL returned 404, so no pack was imported or assumed available. Any later imported pack must be checked for its actual license and animation compatibility before integration.

Current characters use an original articulated hierarchy: hips, spine, head, shoulders, elbows, knees, hands and shoes. Skin tones, clothes, headwraps and caps vary. Their motion is procedural. These are improved stylized low-poly characters, not photorealistic scanned people or production-quality skinned meshes.

## Technical reference
[PlayCanvas standalone engine guidance](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/engine/standalone/) supports the local runtime workflow. The local launcher currently loads the official stable engine CDN (observed version 2.7.4 during testing). Pin or vendor that dependency before a production release.

## 0.4 research and dependencies
- [Alternative Urbanism — La Biennale / Oshinowo Studio](https://www.labiennale.org/en/architecture/2025/collective/alternative-urbanism-self-organized-markets-lagos): research context for distinct specialized market spaces. The game district is fictional, not a copied real city plan.
- [PlayCanvas audio guidance](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/audio/): browser audio requires user interaction. Applied as the Begin Shift audio unlock and explicit controls.
- [PlayCanvas ray casting](https://developer.playcanvas.com/user-manual/physics/ray-casting/): consulted for collision-query concepts. Current implementation uses its own swept segment/body tests and static obstacle sampling; it does not claim to use Ammo raycasts.
- Engine 2.7.4 is now vendored under `dist/vendor/`, with [upstream MIT license](https://github.com/playcanvas/engine/blob/v2.7.4/LICENSE). No remote CDN is required to play the current standalone build.
- All sound effects are generated by original Web Audio code. Optional voice uses browser speech synthesis. No third-party audio samples or voice impersonations are included.
